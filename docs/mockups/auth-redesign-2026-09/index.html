Three sign-in flows for smelter

Every version drops the in-app Create Account screen, the name-plus-PIN sign-in and the invite-triggers-signup bug. Google and Apple are the front door; email and password stays for people who want it; account creation lives on smelterpos.com inside an in-app browser. Scroll each strip sideways to walk the flow.

AFront door · Studio light BOne field · Black CPick your path · Studio light Verdict
Recommendation

Build A. Borrow B's routing later if people keep tapping the wrong button.

A is the flow every employee has already seen in other apps, so nobody needs teaching. It needs the least new backend (OAuth providers plus an email on the invite), it passes App Store 4.8 with Apple next to Google, and the invite path is two taps from launch. B is the smartest but needs an account-lookup endpoint that leaks which emails exist, and C's welcome screen makes returning users take one extra tap every single day.

Pros

  • Zero learning curve. It is the sign-in screen people already know from every SaaS app.
  • Google is one tap from cold launch, which is what David asked for as the main path.
  • Invite path is linear and short: paste, confirm, choose method, done. No wizard chrome to maintain.
  • Fewest new screens: A2 and A5 replace six existing screens (login, signup, sign-in, secure, secure-pin, secure-password).
  • Studio palette means auth stops feeling like a different app. The SPEC line "auth stays black" flips to "auth uses page/card/accent".

Cons

  • Three stacked buttons plus a card on the launch screen. Minimal, but not sparse. B is sparser.
  • Nothing stops someone whose account is Google-only from trying the email form and getting "wrong password". Copy on the error has to say "This account signs in with Google".
  • Backend: Google and Apple OAuth providers configured in Supabase, native Apple Sign In entitlement, Google iOS client ID. None of this is wired today; authStore has the provider type but no working flow.
  • Backend: invites have invited_name but no email. Either the manager enters an email when creating the invite (recommended, locks A5) or the invitee types it and the app checks it is unused.
  • Backend: PIN sign-in and the name-based lookup get removed. Existing PIN-only employees need a migration path (a one-time "set your password" prompt) before this ships.

Black · today's auth surface, new layout

Identity first. The launch screen has one field. Type your email and the app decides what happens next: password, Google, invite, or "no account". Paste an invite link into that same field and it recognises it. Google and Apple sit underneath for people who never want to type. Keeps the black surface and the red wordmark, which is the strongest brand moment the app has.

Cons

  • Seven screens for a flow A does in six, and the wizard chrome (progress bar, step labels, X) is code to maintain.
  • A sheet with a keyboard on a small phone is tight: the email form in C2 sits under the fold on an iPhone SE until the sheet expands.
  • Backend: same as A: OAuth providers, invite email, PIN retirement.
What changes in code, whichever you pick

Delete app/(auth)/signup.tsx, sign-in.tsx, secure.tsx and secure-pin.tsx with their feature screens. Keep welcome, invite-hello, secure-password and ready and reskin them. Add Google and Apple OAuth in Supabase and the native Apple entitlement. Add an invited email to public.invites so the password screen can bind to it. Retire name-plus-PIN sign-in with a one-time password prompt for existing PIN users. Point "Create an account" at smelterpos.com through expo-web-browser. Version B additionally needs an auth-method lookup endpoint with rate limiting.
